Why MULE

The team needs an environment, not another link.

When the normal stack is missing, a pile of separate tools fails as a pile: a radio with nowhere to put the map, a laptop with nothing to join, and a voice plan that was never written down.

Who it is for

A field team in volunteer disaster response, training, or communications experiments. The planning case is 4–8 authenticated end-user devices on one MULE. They already have phones or laptops. TAK stays on those devices. MULE is the shared box they can still reach.

It is not a single Meshtastic node for a hike, and it is not a replacement for the team’s voice radios or for incident-command nets.

What it refuses

A production spec, a range number, or a user-count maximum. Those need a test article and a log.

What it reuses

Wi-Fi, HaLow, Meshtastic, and TAK-compatible services. The new work is the integration, on one Debian host.

What “done” means

A stranger can build a working node from the repository alone, and can tell a simulation from a measurement.
