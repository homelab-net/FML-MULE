Open · Local-first · Team infrastructure

Bring the network.
Bring the services.

MULE is an open-source team infrastructure node. It combines communications bearers with edge compute, local data, offline maps, TAK-compatible services, and field applications, so a team can keep working when normal infrastructure is unavailable.

The radio isn’t the point

A link moves packets.
MULE brings infrastructure.

MULE reuses existing RF, mesh, and TAK technologies. The engineering problem is integrating communications, compute, storage, identity, local services, graceful degradation, and replaceable hardware into one reproducible team platform.

  1. Multi-bearer networking

    High-rate Wi-Fi, HaLow, LoRa/Meshtastic, Ethernet, and optional WAN serve different parts of the problem. They are not one pipe with a volume knob.

  2. Edge compute and data

    Maps, files, references, and mission data can stay on the node. The scarce thing is RF. The node should not spend it fetching the same map again.

  3. Team services

    TAK-compatible services, files, status, and identity share one Debian host. The link moves packets. The services are why the link exists.

Graceful degradation

Lose capacity, not the entire digital environment.

  1. High-rate Wi-Fivideo · bulk sync · large files
  2. HaLowTAK · maps · voice-class · imagery · web
  3. LoRa / MeshtasticPLI · text · alerts · telemetry
  4. Local-onlymaps · files · references · tools
  5. PACEindependent manual / voice fallback

HaLow is full IP. Voice-class means audio and PTT over IP, the DEGRADED-IP ceiling in FML-ADR-080, not HD video and not the analog voice plan. The access point is required and is not one of these rungs. None of this is hardware-verified.

Why this matters

Portable infrastructure for teams beyond infrastructure.

The target user is not someone shopping for a single radio. It is a team that needs networking, shared data, and useful local services where the normal stack is missing.
